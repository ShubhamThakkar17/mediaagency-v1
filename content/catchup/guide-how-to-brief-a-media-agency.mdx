A media plan is rarely better than the brief behind it. A vague brief produces a generic plan, a round of revisions and a launch that slips. A clear one gets you a sharper plan, faster.

Here is what we ask every client to cover, and the mistakes we see most often.

## The checklist

1. **Objective.** One primary goal, stated plainly. Launch awareness, leads, store footfall, app installs or a shift in perception. If there are two goals, rank them. A plan built for leads looks very different from one built for fame.
2. **Audience.** Who you need to reach, described by life stage, income, behaviour and where they live, not only by age and gender. Say who decides and who influences. In many categories, a parent, a spouse or a dealer matters as much as the buyer.
3. **Markets.** The cities, states or pin codes where the product is available and sales can follow up. Rank them. Name any markets that are off limits.
4. **Budget.** A working media budget, or at least a range. Say whether it includes production, agency fees and taxes. A range of ₹50 L to ₹75 L is far more useful than "we will see what you propose".
5. **Timing.** The launch date, any fixed events and the latest date for sign-off. Hoardings, print positions and festive inventory need lead time, so the earlier we know, the better the sites and the rates.
6. **KPIs.** How success will be judged and who will judge it. Enquiries, cost per qualified lead, branded search lift, site visits or sales. Share past benchmarks if you have them, even rough ones.
7. **Creative assets.** What already exists and what needs to be made. Master visuals, films, brand guidelines, language versions and formats. Say who owns creative if it is not us.
8. **Approvals.** Who signs off the plan, the creative and the invoices, and how long each step takes. Name one point of contact on your side.

## What else helps

- **Past campaigns.** What you ran, what it cost and what you think worked. Honest failures are as useful as successes.
- **Competitors.** Who you lose business to, and where you see them advertising.
- **Sales reality.** How leads are handled, how fast the team calls back and what a good lead looks like.
- **Constraints.** Regulatory rules for your category, legal review times and any channels you will not use.

## Common mistakes

- **Too many objectives.** Awareness, leads and loyalty in one six-week burst usually means none of them done well.
- **No budget.** Asking for a plan "without limits" wastes a round. We will build to a number either way, so it may as well be yours.
- **Choosing channels before the plan.** A brief that says "we want hoardings and social" skips the thinking. Tell us the problem and let the plan earn its channels.
- **KPIs that do not match the media.** Judging a print and outdoor launch on next-day sales sets everyone up to fail. Agree measures that fit what each channel does, such as branded search lift for outdoor and cost per lead for search and social.
- **Late approvals.** A plan approved a week late often loses the best sites and the best print positions.
- **No one owns the lead.** If enquiries sit unanswered for two days, the media will look worse than it was.
