A ₹25 lakh launch budget is serious money for a growing business. It is also small enough to disappear if it is spread across too many channels. The job is not to be everywhere. The job is to be unmissable to the right people in one place, for long enough to matter.

Here is how our team approaches it.

## Start with one market, not one country

The first decision is geography. ₹25 L can dominate one tier 1 city or a cluster of tier 2 towns for six to eight weeks. It cannot do the same for a state.

Pick the market where your product is available, your sales team is ready and your early buyers live. Winning one city gives you proof and cash flow for the next.

## Give every rupee one of two jobs

Each channel in a launch plan should do one of two things.

- **Create demand.** Make people aware that you exist and give them a reason to care. Hoardings, print, radio and video do this.
- **Capture demand.** Be present when interested people look for you, and turn that interest into an enquiry. Search and social lead campaigns do this.

A launch that only creates demand hands its enquiries to competitors. A launch that only captures demand has very little to capture. For most categories we begin near a 60:40 split in favour of demand creation, then adjust as data arrives.

## A worked example

Take a consumer launch in one tier 1 city over six weeks. A sensible starting split looks like this. Every figure is an indicative planning number, not a rate card.

- **Hoardings: ₹8 L.** Eight to ten strong sites on the main approach roads and junctions. This is what makes the brand look established.
- **Print: ₹6 L.** A launch day ad in a leading daily, with follow-up insertions over the next two weekends.
- **Search: ₹4 L.** Always on from day one, so nobody who looks for you comes away empty-handed.
- **Social: ₹4 L.** Video for reach in the first fortnight, then lead campaigns and retargeting.
- **Radio: ₹2 L.** A two-week burst around launch day to add frequency on the commute.
- **Reserve: ₹1 L.** Held back until week three.

That is five channels, each funded above its minimum effective level. Notice what is missing: television, celebrity fees and airport media. They are fine channels, but each needs more than this budget can spare.

## Respect the minimums

Every medium has a floor below which it does not register. One hoarding on a quiet road, two radio spots a day or a single small print ad are not half as good as a proper campaign. They are close to wasted.

If the budget cannot fund a channel properly, drop the channel. Four channels done well beat eight done thinly. Our Media Planner applies these floors automatically, which is why it sometimes recommends fewer channels than you expected.

## Phase the spend

Do not spread money evenly across the weeks. We usually plan three phases.

1. **Weeks 1 and 2: announce.** Outdoor goes up, print and radio break, and social video runs at full weight.
2. **Weeks 3 and 4: convert.** Offline spends taper. Search and social lead campaigns take a larger share.
3. **Weeks 5 and 6: sustain.** Retargeting, a reminder print ad and the reserve, directed to whatever has worked best.

## Decide in advance how you will judge it

Agree the numbers before the first rupee is spent. For most launches we track enquiries, cost per qualified lead, branded search volume and walk-ins or site visits.

Review weekly. Move money from what is weak to what is strong, and keep the reserve for that purpose. A launch plan is a starting hypothesis. The brands that get the most from ₹25 L are the ones that treat week three as a chance to rewrite it.
