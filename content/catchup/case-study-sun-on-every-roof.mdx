This is a concept case study. The client is fictional and the numbers are illustrative.

## The brief

Suryoday Solar installs rooftop solar systems for homes. Its problem was not awareness of solar. Most homeowners in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh had heard of it. The problem was trust. People were unsure what a system really cost after the subsidy, who to believe, and whether a young company would still be around to service the panels in five years.

The brief was to generate qualified leads at a cost the sales team could sustain, across three states and two languages.

## The plan

A rooftop system is a considered purchase. Families discuss it, ask the neighbours and check the numbers more than once. So the plan had three stages: build trust, explain the money, then capture the enquiry.

The mix:

- **Print.** Vernacular dailies in Gujarati and Hindi, with district editions in towns where electricity bills are high and roofs are large. Long-copy ads explained the system in plain language.
- **Buses and autos.** State transport bus branding on intercity and district routes, carrying the brand into towns that city media miss.
- **Digital PR.** Explainers placed with regional news sites and personal finance writers on how the subsidy works, what paperwork is needed and how long payback takes.
- **Search.** Campaigns on rooftop solar, subsidy and installer searches in each state, sending traffic to a simple savings calculator and a call-back form.

## The work on the ground

We wrote the subsidy story once, checked it carefully with the client's team and then used it everywhere. The print ads, the PR explainers and the landing pages all gave the same steps in the same order. Consistency was the point. Confused buyers do not call.

Bus routes were chosen with the client's installer network. There was no value in creating demand in a district where no team could reach a roof within a week.

Every lead was called back by someone who spoke the customer's language, and the sales team marked each one qualified or not within 48 hours. That flag fed back into search bidding, so money moved towards the keywords and districts that produced real installations, not just form fills.

## What happened

Over 16 weeks the campaign produced about 11,000 qualified leads at an average of ₹410 per qualified lead. Among leads who said how they had heard of the company, 38% named one of the subsidy explainers.

The pattern was clear. Print and buses made the name familiar. PR gave buyers a reason to believe the numbers. Search was where they finally raised their hand.

## What we would do differently

We would bring PR forward. The explainers did their best work once they had been live for a few weeks and were being shared, so starting them alongside the print launch cost us momentum.

We would also add a short radio burst in the hottest months, when electricity bills peak and the savings argument is easiest to make.
