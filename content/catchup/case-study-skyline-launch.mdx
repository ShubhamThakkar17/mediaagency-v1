This is a concept case study. The client is fictional and the numbers are illustrative.

## The brief

Asterix Infra was preparing to launch a residential project on the growth corridor between Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar. The towers were well located, but the site sat a few kilometres off the main road, and the developer was new to buyers in this part of the city.

The brief was direct. Make the project feel established before the first brochure is printed. Fill the site office on launch weekend. Keep the visits coming for the three weeks after that, when most launches go quiet.

## The plan

We built the plan around one fact. Almost every buyer who would consider the project already drove the approach road. So the road became the campaign.

Every rupee had one of two jobs. Hoardings and print would create demand in the catchment. Search and social would capture it and turn it into booked site visits. We weighted the first two weeks towards outdoor and print, then moved money into search and social lead campaigns from week three.

The mix:

- **Hoardings.** A 3 km corridor of sites on the approach road, sequenced so that each one told the next part of the story, ending with a direction board at the turn to the site.
- **Print.** A front-page jacket in a leading Gujarati daily and an English daily on launch weekend, with smaller follow-ups on the next two weekends.
- **Search.** Brand, project and locality keywords live from the day the first hoarding went up.
- **Social.** Video walkthroughs targeted to the pin codes along the corridor and to young professionals in Gandhinagar, then lead forms with a site visit slot built in.

## The work on the ground

The corridor needed careful site selection. Our team drove the route at morning and evening peak, logged sightlines and dropped sites hidden by trees or flyover pillars. We chose fewer, larger sites with clear visibility over a longer list of weaker ones.

Creative was written for the road. One line per site, a large visual of the towers at dusk, and the same image carried into the print jacket and the social videos so that recognition was instant.

Leads went straight to the sales team's phones with a call-back target of 15 minutes. Every visitor at the site office was asked where they had first seen the project, and the answers were logged daily.

## What happened

Within 21 days of launch, the site office logged 1,900 visits. Search and social produced 5,400 enquiries, and branded searches for the project ran at 2.6 times the level of the month before.

The visitor log was the most useful signal. The corridor hoardings were the most common answer to where people had first seen the project, with print close behind on launch weekend. Search and social did most of the converting, but they were converting interest that outdoor and print had created.

## What we would do differently

We would extend the corridor's run by two weeks. Site visits held up well into week three, which suggests the road was still doing work when we scaled it back.

We would also set up a cleaner geo test. A similar catchment with a digital-only plan would have given us a firmer read on how much the hoardings added. Next time we will build that comparison in from the start.
