India's advertising market kept growing in 2025, but the money did not grow evenly. Some media gained ground and one large medium shrank. Here is where the rupee went, using published industry figures, and what we think it means for brands planning 2026.

## The size of the market

According to the Pitch Madison Advertising Report 2026, India's ad spend reached ₹1,55,105 crore in 2025, about 12% more than in 2024. The report uses an expanded definition of ADEX, so its totals should not be compared directly with older editions that measured a narrower set of media.

The same report forecasts ₹1,74,605 crore for 2026. The market is still expanding, and so is the competition for attention.

## Digital keeps pulling ahead

The headline shift is digital. Pitch Madison forecasts digital at about 64% of total ad spend in 2026, or about ₹1,11,976 crore. If the forecast holds, close to two of every three advertising rupees in the country will go to digital media.

That share reflects how measurable and flexible digital is. It also means crowded inventory, so a digital-only plan is not automatically an efficient one.

## Television: still big, but slipping

Linear television remains the largest traditional medium. The Pitch Madison report puts TV ad spend at ₹32,855 crore in 2025, about 5% lower than in 2024. In our view, much of that money is following viewers to connected TV and online video.

For brands, TV still delivers reach at scale, especially in regional markets and with family audiences. The decline is a reason to buy it more precisely, not a reason to drop it.

## Print, outdoor, radio and cinema

The same report puts print at ₹20,866 crore in 2025, outdoor at ₹4,835 crore, radio at ₹2,515 crore and cinema at ₹877 crore.

Print remains a serious medium, particularly regional language dailies, and it suits launches, real estate, education and retail offers. Outdoor is a small slice of national spend, but it is the medium people see in the physical world, every day, at full size. Radio and cinema are niche in rupee terms, which often makes them good value for local and tactical bursts.

## Sports and creators

Two other sources fill in the picture. WPP Media's Sporting Nation report puts India's sports economy at ₹18,864 crore in 2025, covering sponsorship, endorsement and media spends, with cricket accounting for about 89%. Every other sport shares what is left, which can mean better value for brands willing to look beyond cricket.

Kofluence's Decoding Influence: Annual Research Report 2026 estimates influencer marketing at ₹3,000 to 3,500 crore in 2025. That is the publisher's own estimate, but it confirms that creators are now a budget line in their own right.

## What this means for 2026 plans

A few practical points follow from the numbers.

- **Plan digital as the backbone, not the whole plan.** With about 64% of spend forecast to go digital, standing out there costs more. Offline media that build familiarity can make digital work harder and lower the cost of each enquiry.
- **Buy TV with intent.** A shrinking medium can still be the right one. Use regional channels and tight dayparts where the audience is, and pair TV with online video.
- **Use the smaller media where they are strongest.** Outdoor, print, radio and cinema take a modest share of national spend. In a single city or region, they can dominate attention in a way a crowded feed rarely can.
- **Look past the obvious in sport.** Cricket takes most of the money. Other sports and local events can offer presence with far less clutter.
- **Measure across channels.** One report covering spend, delivery and response matters more than any single channel metric.

The market will be bigger in 2026, and busier. The brands that do best will be the ones that know what each rupee is meant to do.
