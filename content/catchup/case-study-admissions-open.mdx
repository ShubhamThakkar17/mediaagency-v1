This is a concept case study. The client is fictional and the numbers are illustrative.

## The brief

Northwind University had a strong name in the metros and a weak one almost everywhere else. Yet its admissions team could see that many of its best students came from smaller towns, often after a parent or teacher had heard of the university by chance.

The brief was to stop leaving that to chance. Reach students and parents in 40 feeder towns across the country, during the short window when families decide, and grow applications from tier 2 towns without inflating the cost of each one.

## The plan

Admissions decisions follow a tight calendar. Board results arrive, families compare options for a few weeks, and applications close. We planned the media to that calendar, not to the financial year.

The mix:

- **Print.** City and district editions of regional and English dailies in all 40 towns, timed to the week results were announced. Parents read results coverage closely, and they keep the paper.
- **Cinema.** On-screen spots in multiplexes and single screens in the feeder towns during the summer holidays, when students go to the cinema with friends and family.
- **Railways.** Station branding at the junctions on the routes students from these towns take to reach the campus and the nearest metro.
- **Social media.** Short video in the local language, with student stories from each region, followed by retargeting to families who had watched or visited the site.

We grouped the 40 towns into eight clusters by language and region, so one set of creative and one buying plan could serve each cluster.

## The work on the ground

Creative was the heaviest lift. We produced versions in six languages, each with a student from that region speaking for the university. The line stayed the same everywhere. The voice changed.

Print insertions were booked well ahead, because results week is a crowded time in the newspaper. Cinema screens were chosen town by town, favouring the halls students actually used over the largest ones. Station sites were picked at platform entrances and ticket halls, where people wait and read.

Every application form asked where the student had first heard of the university, and the answers were tagged by town and cluster.

## What happened

Applications from tier 2 towns rose 31% over the previous admissions cycle. The cost per application fell 22% across the feeder towns, largely because offline media built enough familiarity that social video converted at a better rate.

Print was the source parents named most. Social video and cinema were the sources students named most. Station branding was named less often, but towns with railway presence grew faster than those without it. That is a pattern we want to test properly before we rely on it.

## What we would do differently

We would start social video two weeks before results, not on results day. Students research early, and we missed part of that window.

We would also keep the railway sites up through the counselling rounds. Decisions run later than application deadlines suggest, and the stations stay busy right through the season.
