For many categories in India, the weeks from Navratri to Diwali decide the year. Families buy gold, cars, phones, appliances, clothes and homes. Every brand knows it, so every brand shows up at once. Media gets scarce, prices rise and attention is split many ways.

Here is how our team plans the season.

## Book earlier than feels necessary

The best festive inventory goes first. Prime hoardings on the main shopping roads, front-page and jacket positions in festive editions, and premium screens in malls are often committed well before the season starts.

As a rule of thumb, we start conversations with media owners three to four months ahead for outdoor and premium print, and lock key sites at least eight to ten weeks before the first festival. Digital is more flexible, but audiences and creative still need time to set up and test. These are indicative lead times. They vary by city and by year.

## Expect the crunch

Demand for inventory peaks in the weeks before Dussehra and Diwali. Availability matters more than the rate card, and the price of a good site or position can rise well above its usual level. Treat any cost range you see for this period as indicative, and confirm it in writing when you book.

Two habits help. Hold a reserve, so you can pick up late inventory when another advertiser drops out. And be willing to trade the most famous site for two strong ones nearby.

## Plan by region, not by nation

India does not celebrate on one calendar. Navratri and garba nights dominate Gujarat. Durga Puja takes over West Bengal and much of the east. Dussehra is the big moment in Karnataka and across the north. Onam arrives earlier in Kerala. Chhath follows Diwali in Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh, and the wedding season starts soon after.

So build a calendar for each region. A national flight that peaks on one date will be early for some markets and late for others. Phase the bursts to each region's festival, and adapt creative to its language and rituals.

## The mix that works

Each channel has a clear job in the festive plan.

- **Outdoor.** Hoardings and digital screens on shopping corridors and near malls create presence where buying happens. Digital OOH lets you change the message as the season moves, from early offers to countdowns to Diwali greetings.
- **Print.** Festive editions are read closely and kept. Print suits offers, store lists, financing details and anything that needs space. Regional language dailies carry particular weight.
- **Social.** Short video builds anticipation before the festival and keeps the brand present through it. Target the pin codes around your stores and dealers, and refresh creative often, because festive feeds are crowded.
- **Search.** Searches for offers, prices and nearby stores climb through the season. Keep brand and category campaigns funded through the peak days, when it is most tempting to cut, and make sure store locations and timings are correct.

Run them as one plan. The visual on the hoarding should match the one in the paper and the feed, and search should be ready to catch the interest the rest creates.

## Measure the season properly

Set baselines before the season starts: footfall, enquiries, branded search and sales from a normal month. Then compare by region and by week. Festive demand lifts everything, so the question is not whether sales rose but whether your share of the season grew.

## The short version

Book early, plan by region, hold a reserve and run every channel as one campaign. The festive season rewards the brands that finish their planning while others are still starting.
