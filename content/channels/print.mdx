## How it works in India

India is still a newspaper country. Dailies are published in English, Hindi and more than a dozen regional languages, and most are sold as city or district editions. That means you can buy one edition for one catchment or stack editions to cover a state.

Rates are quoted per square centimetre or by fixed sizes such as quarter page, half page and full page. Position matters. A front page or page three placement costs more than a run-of-paper slot, and weekend issues often carry a premium.

## When it earns its place

Print works when the message needs authority, detail or a date.

- A project launch, a new hospital wing or a showroom opening.
- Admission and results season for schools, colleges and coaching.
- Festive and wedding season offers for retail and jewellery.
- Public notices, financial results and issue advertising.

Readers give a newspaper time and trust. A large ad in a respected daily tells a family or a business owner that the brand is serious.

## How we plan and buy it

We start with the catchment, not the title. Our team maps where your buyers live, then picks the editions and languages that reach them with the least waste.

We plan frequency in short bursts around release days, usually 3 to 5 insertions rather than one large splash. We negotiate position, package deals across editions and value additions such as advertorial space. Every release carries a response hook so results can be read edition by edition.

## What to watch

Print is not a medium for one-off experiments. A single small insertion rarely pays back. Creative must be built for newsprint, with bold headlines, limited copy and a clear call to action. Readership among younger audiences is low, so pair print with social or cinema when Gen Z is part of the target. Costs quoted here are indicative planning ranges and move with season and position.
