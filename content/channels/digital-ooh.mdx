## How it works in India

Digital screens have spread quickly through Indian cities. You will find them at traffic junctions, in mall atriums, office tower lobbies, apartment lifts and cafes. Most are sold as networks, so one buy can cover hundreds of screens across a city.

You buy a slot, typically 10 seconds in a loop shared with other advertisers. Some networks can now be bought programmatically, with triggers such as time of day or weather.

## When it earns its place

- Short campaigns where printing and mounting would eat the timeline.
- Offers that change by day, such as weekend sales or match-day promotions.
- Reaching professionals and affluent households in places they visit daily.
- Adding motion and freshness to a static outdoor plan.

It is an urban medium. The best inventory sits in the metros and the larger tier 1 cities of the West and South.

## How we plan and buy it

We choose environments first. A corporate park screen and a residential lift screen reach very different mindsets, so the message should differ too.

Our team plans dayparts, with breakfast offers in the morning and dinner offers in the evening. We check screen brightness, loop length and share of voice before we commit. We ask for play-out logs as standard and audit a sample of screens ourselves.

## What to watch

You share the screen, so a long loop means few plays an hour. Ask how many advertisers are in the loop. Screen quality varies, and a dim screen in daylight is close to invisible. Outside the top cities, inventory thins out fast, which is where hoardings and transit media take over. The cost ranges shown are indicative and depend heavily on location quality.
