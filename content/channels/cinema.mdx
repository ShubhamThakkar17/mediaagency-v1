## How it works in India

India loves the movies. Multiplex chains dominate the metros and large cities, while single screens still matter in the South and in smaller towns. Advertising runs before the film and during the interval, which is a uniquely Indian advantage: the audience is seated and waiting.

Inventory is sold per screen per week. Rates depend on the city, the property and the films releasing that week. Off-screen media includes the lobby, box office, concession counters and seats.

## When it earns its place

- Brands that want Gen Z and young professionals with full attention.
- Launch films that deserve a big screen and proper sound.
- Catchment campaigns around a mall or high street.
- Regional pushes timed to a major regional language release.

Cinema is especially strong in the South, where film culture is deep and screens are many.

## How we plan and buy it

We plan around the release calendar. Our team picks the weeks with strong films in the right languages, then selects screens by catchment and audience profile.

We prefer fewer properties with every show covered over a thin spread across a city. Where the budget allows, we add foyer presence so the brand is seen before and after the film. The same creative can then be cut for social to extend its life.

## What to watch

Admissions swing with the film slate, so a weak release week delivers a weak audience. Reach is modest compared with television, and coverage in tier 3 and rural markets is limited. A film made for mobile may look poor on a large screen, so check the master. Treat the cost ranges here as indicative. Big release weeks are priced at a premium and sell out early.
