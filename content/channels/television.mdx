## How it works in India

Television reaches more Indian homes than any other paid medium. Viewing is split across genres: general entertainment, news, movies, music, kids and sports. Regional language channels are powerful in the South, West Bengal, Maharashtra and the Hindi belt.

Airtime is sold in 10 second units. The price depends on the channel, the programme and the time band. Prime time and big live events cost many times more than daytime. Connected TV adds addressable buying in urban homes, city by city.

## When it earns its place

- A national or multi-state launch that needs reach in weeks, not months.
- Categories bought by the household, such as FMCG, durables, two-wheelers and insurance.
- Brands expanding into tier 2, tier 3 and rural markets, where TV remains the family screen.
- Festive and big cricket season bursts.

Television also changes how a brand is perceived. Being on TV signals scale to consumers, dealers and distributors alike.

## How we plan and buy it

We set a reach and frequency goal first, then build the channel mix to hit it at the lowest cost. That usually means a few anchor programmes for impact and a wide base of efficient spots for frequency.

Our team buys regional feeds where a national feed would waste money. We negotiate bonus spots and sponsorship tags, and we check every spot against the monitoring log. Shorter edits take over once the story is established.

## What to watch

TV is weak at direct response, so do not judge it on leads alone. Fragmented viewing means a thin plan disappears. It is better to dominate two genres in one region than to scatter spots nationally. Young urban viewers are moving to streaming, so add connected TV, social or cinema to reach them. All cost figures here are indicative planning ranges, not rate cards.
