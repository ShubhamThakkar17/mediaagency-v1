## How it works in India

Metro rail now runs in a growing list of Indian cities, led by the big networks in the North, West and South. Each metro corporation licenses train exteriors, usually through a media concessionaire, and sets strict rules on materials and content.

A wrap covers the outside of a whole train or selected coaches in printed vinyl. It is rented per train per month. Printing and installation are a significant one-time cost.

## When it earns its place

A wrap is a statement piece.

- A brand launching in a metro city that wants to look established from the first day.
- A real estate, fintech or auto brand claiming a corridor.
- A campaign that needs something photographed, shared and talked about.

Elevated lines give the wrap a second audience: everyone in traffic and in buildings along the route.

## How we plan and buy it

We pick the line before the train. Our team studies which corridor connects the offices, colleges and neighbourhoods you want, and how much of it runs above ground.

We design for speed and distance, with huge branding, bold colour and very few words. We manage authority approvals and depot installation, and we plan interior panels or station media alongside so the idea continues inside the coach.

## What to watch

This is not a small-budget or short-notice medium. Approvals can take time and content rules are strict. Trains are rotated across the line, so you cannot fix a wrap to particular stations or hours. Underground stretches reduce exterior visibility, which makes line choice important. Wraps exist only where metros run, so they play no role in tier 2 and tier 3 plans.
