## How it works in India

Every metro network sells media inside its stations and trains. Stations offer backlit panels, pillar wraps, digital screens and promotional kiosks. Coaches offer interior panels above the windows, beside the doors and on grab handles. Some stations can carry a brand name alongside their own.

Media is sold as station packages or line packages by the month. Prices follow footfall, with interchange and business district stations at the top.

## When it earns its place

- Brands aimed at salaried professionals and students.
- Categories that need a little explanation, such as fintech, insurance, courses and apps.
- Businesses near a station that want to own their catchment.
- Sustained campaigns where daily repetition does the work.

The same commuter sees the message on the way in and on the way home, often for weeks.

## How we plan and buy it

We begin with station footfall and catchment profiles. A tech park station and an old city market station call for different brands and different copy.

Our team builds a package that follows the commuter journey: entry, concourse, platform and coach. We adapt creative to each touchpoint, with a bold version for walking spaces and a detailed one for coach interiors. We add QR codes or short links wherever people are standing still.

## What to watch

It reaches only metro users, which in most cities is a specific slice of the population. Peak hour crowding can block low panels, so placement height matters. Content approvals follow the metro authority's rules and can add days. Networks are limited to the larger cities, so this medium has no role in a Bharat plan. Costs here are indicative planning ranges and vary by station.
