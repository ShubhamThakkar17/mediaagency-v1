Marketing teams often treat outdoor and digital as rivals for the same budget. One is seen as old and hard to measure. The other is seen as modern and accountable. In practice they do different jobs, and they do them better as a pair.

## Two halves of one journey

A hoarding does something a phone screen cannot. It makes a brand part of the city. People see it at the same junction every day, at full size, with no skip button. That repetition builds familiarity and a sense that the brand is real and substantial.

What a hoarding cannot do is take an enquiry. Nobody fills in a form at a traffic signal. They go home, pick up a phone and search. That is the moment digital exists for.

So the journey has two halves. Outdoor creates the interest. Search and social capture it. Fund only the first and the enquiries leak to whoever is bidding on your category. Fund only the second and you are fishing in a small pond.

## What changes when you plan them together

When the two are planned as one campaign, a few practical things happen.

- **Search is ready on day one.** Brand and category campaigns go live the day the first site is mounted, so the lift in searches lands on your page.
- **Social follows the map.** Paid social is targeted to the same pin codes and corridors as the outdoor sites. People who saw the hoarding meet the brand again in their feed.
- **Creative matches.** The same visual, colours and line appear on the street and on the screen. Recognition is instant, and that tends to improve click-through.
- **Timing is shared.** The digital burst is weighted to the weeks when outdoor is at full strength.

## Digital screens bridge the gap

Digital OOH sits between the two worlds. It has the public presence of outdoor and some of the flexibility of online media. Creative can change by time of day. A campaign can go live in days because nothing needs printing.

We often use static hoardings for constant presence on arterial roads and digital screens for tactical messages in malls, office parks and residential towers. A countdown before launch, an offer on the weekend and a thank-you afterwards can all run on the same screens.

## Measuring the pair

Outdoor is harder to measure than digital, but it is far from unmeasurable. These are the checks we rely on.

1. **Branded search lift.** Compare search volume for the brand name in the campaign city before, during and after the burst.
2. **Geo comparison.** Run outdoor in one catchment and not in a similar one, with the same digital plan in both. Compare leads and cost per lead.
3. **Direct response hooks.** A QR code, a short link or a dedicated phone number on the creative gives a floor, though never the full picture.
4. **Lead quality.** Ask the sales team where enquiries say they saw the brand.

When the two channels run together, we look for more branded searches and a steadier cost per lead in the areas with outdoor presence. We treat that as a test to run, not a promise. Results differ by category and city.

## How to split the money

There is no universal ratio. As a starting point for a city launch, we often put 50 to 60 percent of the combined budget into outdoor and the rest into search and social, then rebalance after the first three weeks. These are indicative planning figures.

For scale, the Pitch Madison Advertising Report 2026 puts outdoor at ₹4,835 crore of India's 2025 ad spend, and forecasts digital at about 64 percent of the total in 2026. Digital takes the larger share of budgets. Outdoor remains the part of the plan people see in the physical world.

## The takeaway

Stop asking whether outdoor or digital deserves the budget. Ask what each must do for the other. Put the brand on the road, then make sure you are the first result when people reach for their phones.
